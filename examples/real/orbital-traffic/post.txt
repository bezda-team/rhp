TITLE
[OC] Below 600 km, 95% of publicly catalogued objects are spacecraft. At 600 to 1,100 km, 71% are debris.

BODY
The mix of objects in low Earth orbit changes sharply with altitude.

In this public-catalogue snapshot, spacecraft account for 13,912 of the 14,571 objects below 600 km. In the next band, from 600 to 1,100 km, debris accounts for 7,632 of 10,686 objects.

Here, spacecraft means the catalogue's payload category, including both working and inactive spacecraft. Debris means its DEB category; rocket bodies and unknown classifications are counted as Other.

The symbols sit at representative altitudes and each stands for about 750 objects. Their angular positions and sizes are illustrative. The percentages use the full counts.

This is a comparison of the public catalogue, not everything in orbit. Most small debris is missing, and these percentages do not measure collision risk.

FIRST COMMENT
Source: CelesTrak's public SATCAT, retrieved October 18, 2026.
https://celestrak.org/pub/satcat.csv

Field definitions:
https://celestrak.org/satcat/satcat-format.php

I filtered for Earth-centred ORB entries with no decay date and published altitudes satisfying 0 <= perigee <= apogee < 2,000 km. Each object's altitude band uses the midpoint of its apogee and perigee. That is an altitude summary, not its instantaneous position; elliptical orbits can cross band boundaries. The upper limits of the bands are exclusive.

The headline values round 95.477% and 71.421% to whole percentages. Exact public-catalogue counts are used for the readout; the pictograms are rounded separately within each category and band.

Analysis: Python. Chart and interactive version: rhp with HTML/CSS. Earth and hardware are generated illustrative artwork, styled as a vintage science-fiction poster, not photographs of specific catalogue objects.

The image is not a map of individual positions, an inventory of uncatalogued debris, or a safety ranking.
