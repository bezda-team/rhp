[OC] Sulfur dioxide fell 94% at EPA-tracked U.S. power plants, while electricity output fell 2% (2000 to 2023)

Suggested first top-level comment
Data: U.S. EPA, Progress Report, Emissions Reductions, Figure 4.
https://www.epa.gov/power-sector/progress-report-emissions-reductions#so2

Tools: rhp (@bezda/rhp), HTML/CSS, JavaScript, and Playwright for visual checks.
The rain-on-glass alternate uses an AI-generated decorative background made with OpenAI image generation.
The background does not represent measured acid rain.

I summed the coal, gas, oil and other fuel series separately for sulfur dioxide and electricity generation, then indexed each total to 100 in 2000.
Sulfur dioxide went from 11.20 million to 0.65 million U.S. short tons, a 94.2% decline.
Generation went from 2,476 to 2,430 TWh, a 1.9% decline.
The two lines share one index axis, rather than using two different vertical axes.

Scope: plants covered in each year's Acid Rain Program and CSAPR SO2 programs, not every U.S. power plant.
CSAPR-only SO2 units enter in 2015, so this is not a fixed panel of identical plants.
EPA attributes the decline to pollution controls and lower-emitting fuels; it does not assign the whole reduction to one policy or technology.

The smaller 73% figure is a separate EPA finding: wet sulfate deposition in the eastern U.S. fell between the 2000 to 2002 and 2020 to 2022 averages.
It is not a percentage change in rainfall pH.
https://www.epa.gov/power-sector/progress-report-atmospheric-deposition

Recovery remains uneven, particularly in central Appalachian streams.
https://www.epa.gov/power-sector/progress-report-ecosystem-response

I used the internally consistent historical series through 2023 published in this EPA progress report.
This does not describe conditions in 2026, and it is not a claim that acid rain has disappeared.
