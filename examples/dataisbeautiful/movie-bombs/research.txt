Omissions and alternative claims
Desert Warrior's approximately $149M budget/gross gap is not a net-loss forecast.
Masters of the Universe's FilmDB $140M to $150M claim is derived from production cost and generic cinema rentals, without a sourced first-cycle model including later income.
No comparable numerical first-cycle forecast for those films was verified in the original trade reports reviewed.
The Mandalorian and Grogu was also checked in original Deadline, Hollywood Reporter and Variety coverage, without verifying a comparable numerical forecast.
The $100M theatrical-loss sentence in Variety's Disney CEO article refers to Moana, not The Mandalorian and Grogu.
Variety's 30 September comparison gives theatrical-only estimates of $100M for The Bride!, $80M for Supergirl, $25M to $40M for The End of Oak Street, and $30M to $40M for Practical Magic 2.
The article also records a source disputing the last estimate and forecasting a $5M profit.
https://variety.com/2026/film/box-office/digger-tom-cruise-warner-bros-box-office-1236878000/
Those estimates are recorded in the audit and are not blended into the first-cycle comparison.
The Dog Stars has an unbounded theatrical-loss description, rather than a precise comparable forecast.
The Sheep Detectives has a generic theatrical calculator model, which is excluded.
The audit lists source URLs, reasons and the other searched candidates.

Dates and uncertainty
These are original dated forecasts, not new forecasts as of October.
Hatching spans the quoted estimates, not statistical confidence bounds.
The Bride! retains approximate language, and Supergirl's point forecast is not given a fabricated range.
Moana's later $323.7M worldwide gross exceeds its forecast's $250M assumption.
Digger's $40.8M gross exceeds its report's maximum $40M assumption and the film remains in release.
Supergirl's Deadline report gives no final-gross assumption, so Variety's separate $200M assumption is not imported.
Forecasts include post-theatrical income, but their unpublished models and report dates still differ.
No loss is recomputed with a generic rental percentage, and no total or precise ranking of final outcomes is claimed.
The credits identify directors and stars, without attributing financial causation or artistic quality to them.

Audience evidence and its limits
A recent r/dataisbeautiful post about Ridley Scott's film finances generated a substantial discussion of familiar directors, particular movies, streaming revenue, and accounting assumptions.
The search result displayed 1,910 votes and a 14 September 2026 publication date, but the opened page used inconsistent relative dates and did not expose a stable current score.
No exact score or posting date is used as a verified engagement claim.
https://www.reddit.com/r/dataisbeautiful/comments/1wg1eiz/oc_ridley_scotts_filmography_as_boxoffice/
Readers challenged incomplete global grosses, treating streaming receipts as zero, and implying career profitability from cinema-only results.
Those objections inform this chart's explicit forecast dates, source disagreements, and revenue-window labels.
A separate recent Nolan budget-versus-gross post is another relevant precedent.
https://www.reddit.com/r/dataisbeautiful/comments/1v845un/christopher_nolan_budget_vs_box_office_oc/
The current Digger news supplies a more immediate hook than an old film's financial history.
Interest looks plausible from the recognizable cast, current release, and existing discussion, but these observations do not predict how many votes this draft will receive.
The biggest editorial weakness is that original forecasts are uncertain and come from different dates and undisclosed accounting assumptions.
The chart and source comment disclose that weakness and limit the ranking to the comparable forecasts verified in the source review.
