FIRST TOP-LEVEL COMMENT

Data: Valve's public Steam review API for No Man's Sky (app 275850), collected on 17 October 2026 UTC, with reviews through 16 October 2026 UTC.
Steam page: https://store.steampowered.com/app/275850/No_Mans_Sky/
API and field definitions: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/webapi/IUserReviewsService

Tools: JavaScript for collection and aggregation; rhp (@bezda/rhp) for the HTML chart; modern-screenshot for the PNG export; Playwright for browser and image checks.

I grouped all 423,186 public reviews by their original posting year, across all languages and purchase types, including off-topic activity.
The 2016 group contains 29,894 of the 62,201 current negative recommendations, or 48.1%.
Reviews first posted since 2020 are 92.3% positive.
Both sides of the chart use the same linear scale, and the labels give exact counts.
The 2026 row is a partial year.

One important distinction: reviewers can edit their recommendations.
These are current thumbs-up/down votes grouped by when the review was first posted, not archived ratings from those years.
For example, a review first posted in 2016 and changed to positive in 2026 counts as a positive review in the 2016 row.
This does not tell us how many individual players changed their minds or prove that an update caused a change.

The annual counts were checked against a separate full-period API query and reconcile exactly.
This includes reviews from key activations and other non-Steam purchases, so its totals need not match the Steam-purchaser score displayed at the top of the store page.

I was interested in how much the launch still contributes to the review record after ten years of updates.
Do you usually look at a game's recent reviews or its all-time score when deciding to play it?
